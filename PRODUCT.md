# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

Tech recruiters at companies offering software engineering co-ops and internships. They arrive via LinkedIn or a referral link, spend under 30 seconds forming a first impression, and are looking for two things: competence (does this person ship?) and personality (would I want to work with them?). A secondary audience of senior engineers and hiring managers who dig deeper into projects and architecture decisions.

## Product Purpose

Personal portfolio for Gahan Patel, a CS and Business student at Northeastern University. The site's job is to convert recruiter visits into co-op interview invites. Success looks like: a recruiter opens the page, reads the name, skims the work, and clicks the resume or email link within 30 seconds. The design needs to signal both technical seriousness and enough creative personality to stand out in a stack of identical portfolios.

## Brand Personality

Precise but human. The site should feel like someone who writes clean code and has good taste — not a template, not a showcase of visual tricks. Three words: **considered, capable, real**.

Gahan's tagline ("Building things that matter — software that scales, systems that ship") sets the voice: direct, confident, no filler. The design should honor that without tipping into cold or over-engineered.

## Anti-references

- **Generic dev portfolio template**: The indigo-hero + skills-progress-bars + identical-card-grid look that every bootcamp portfolio uses. The current design is already at risk of this pattern.
- **Neon / dark cyberpunk**: Glowing borders, heavy gradients, high-contrast neon on black. Looks performative rather than considered.
- **Linear / Vercel clone**: Dark, precise tool-builder aesthetic is the right reference feel, but copying it wholesale produces a portfolio that looks like every other engineer who idolizes those products. The refinement is the goal; the exact palette and typographic choices are not.
- **Over-minimal / blank**: So stripped-back it disappears — just a name, title, and link list. Forgettable.

## Design Principles

1. **The site is the argument.** Every design decision makes a case for hiring Gahan. Weak hierarchy, flat sections, or decorative noise all subtract from that argument.
2. **Personality through restraint.** Character comes through in small, deliberate moments — a distinctive type pairing, an unexpected color decision, a section that doesn't look like the others — not through big visual gestures.
3. **Scannable in 10 seconds.** Recruiters shouldn't have to hunt. The hierarchy does the work: name, role, one-line bio, work. The eye should fall through in order.
4. **Trust the work.** Projects and experience carry the weight. The design frames them without competing with them.
5. **Craft signals craft.** The site itself is a work sample. Sloppy spacing, generic choices, and untouched defaults undermine the candidate's credibility as much as a weak project would.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

No specific compliance target. Semantic HTML, keyboard navigation, and sufficient contrast as standard practice. Animations should be meaningful, not gratuitous.
